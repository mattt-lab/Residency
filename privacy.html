Privacy policy

Effective date: October 21, 2026

The short version: everything stays on your iPhone

Residency works out every day on your iPhone. The developer has no servers and no accounts, so nothing about you, your location, your travel or your photos is ever sent to us, and nothing we do reads your data from your iPhone or your iCloud. Residency collects no data.

The only copies of your records are on your iPhone and in your own iCloud (the app’s backup), which belong to you. When the app goes online, it goes straight to the service doing the work for you, never through us: Apple (a border check, described below, and purchases), TripIt if you connect it, and your iCloud for your backup.

Your location and travel data

The places you have been, your day counts and your settings are stored on your iPhone. If iCloud Drive is on, the app also keeps a backup in its own folder in your iCloud, so you can restore after a new phone or a reinstall; only your iPhone writes and reads it. If you use iCloud Backup or back up your iPhone to a computer, the app’s data is included in that backup too. All of these belong to you and are handled by Apple or by you. The developer cannot see them.

Location is used on the device to work out which state or country you are in. It is never sent to the developer. When a reading is close to a state or country border, the app may ask Apple’s location service (the one Apple Maps uses) which side of the border it is on; Apple’s privacy policy applies to that request.

States, not addresses

The app works out the state or country from your location, then keeps only the state, the time and a location rounded to about 7 miles. It keeps no addresses and no map of where you have been. Every day can be corrected, and a day you corrected is labelled as your own entry in reports.

Photos

If you choose to fill in days from your photos, the app asks for access to your photo library. It reads only the date and place of photos you took, on your iPhone, to work out which state or country you were in. It keeps that place and time like a location reading, never the photos themselves. Photos and their places are never sent to the developer. To show you a photo, the app asks Apple’s Photos on your iPhone for it, and Photos may download it from your own iCloud Photos. You can remove the places it kept at any time in Settings, and turn off its access in iPhone Settings.

TripIt

If you choose to connect TripIt, you sign in directly with TripIt inside a web view in the app. The sign-in goes to TripIt, not to the developer, and the app never sees your password. The app then reads trips from your own TripIt account and stores them on your iPhone. The address of your TripIt calendar feed, if you add it, is stored in the iPhone Keychain. TripIt's own privacy policy applies to your TripIt account.

Purchases

In-app purchases are processed by Apple. The free trial needs no sign-up or payment. The developer receives no personal information about you from these purchases.

Feedback emails

If you send feedback, the app opens an email draft. It includes the app version, iOS version and iPhone model so problems can be looked into, plus whatever you write. You can edit or delete anything before sending. Nothing is sent unless you press send.

No analytics, ads or tracking

The app has no analytics, no advertising, no crash-reporting service and no tracking of any kind.

Children

The app is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date.
